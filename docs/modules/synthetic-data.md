# Synthetic data (`/synthetic-data/`)

Decisions for this module only; the shared rules are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Picture

- **The copier room** (`src/components/scenes/copierScene.ts`): Larkspur's real documents in a stack at the back, the copier (Larkspur mini) in the middle, a sieve on a stand, and along the front a shelf with a stack of copies per generation (0–5). Each top sheet carries 12 rare marks; the lit ones are that generation's rare next-word accuracy against generation 0's, read off `runs.json`, the rest faded. Under accumulate a lit wire feeds the real stack into the copier every generation. `CopierStage` (`src/content/synthetic-data/kit.tsx`) puts it one tab from Numbers, the same runs as a table, with a text read-out of the shown generation's measures under the picture.
- **The mechanic** (KTD15): a native range over generations 0–5 flips the stack (the lit top sheet animates in; with reduced motion it doesn't, and the server draws no flip either, since hydration never patches drawn markup). Chapter 6's prediction is a native two-option radio (replace, accumulate) that locks once picked; a second radio then shows either arm.
- While Larkspur mini loads, the controls that need it are disabled with a status line; on a failed load the frame shows Retry.

## Engine (`src/lib/synthetic-data/`)

- **Live** (`generate.ts`): Larkspur mini writes batches of 20 six-piece replies with Transformers' `sample()` (seed i + 1, top-p 1), a reply per animation frame in the kit. Copies are what dedupe drops; "on topic" is naming a product. The teacher answers the nine rare prompts greedily, graded by Evals' `keyFactOf`.
- **The sieve** (`sieve.ts`) is `train_synth.py`'s rule: exact copy, then 4-gram overlap of at least half with kept samples, then the fact filter (a product name's ids from `ids.json` `syntheticData`).
- **Recorded** (`runs.ts`, `data/synthetic-data/runs.json`, 47 KB, no weights): `scripts/train/train_synth.py` trains each generation from scratch, Larkspur mini's shape, 3,000 steps, seeded and byte-identical on rerun. Two arms of five generations (replace, accumulate) and the 18-run grid (temperature {0.7, 1, 1.3} × sieve {off, dedupe, dedupe + facts} × real share {0, 50%}). Per run: held-out loss, top-5 and rare top-5 (held-out positions whose next piece is in the rarest half by training count), distinct rare pieces used, repeat rate, greedy samples as ids.
- **The re-scope** (decided with the user): the plan's tail measure (rare-token share) and then distinct rare words were tried and failed on the neural runs. Under replace the copies use *more* distinct rare pieces (181 → 351), many never in the real text: they invent, not forget. That check is kept in `runs.json` as `vanishing`, not gated. The module teaches drift instead: held-out loss rises every generation under replace (4.04 → 5.88) and stays near the start under accumulate (4.33), and rare-word guesses fall fastest (27% → 5% replace, 14% accumulate; all words 44% → 24%). Gates in the script and `tests/unit/synthetic-data-runs.test.ts`: drift, rare drift, rare words first, repeat rate under 0.10.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a recipe is a grid run; it wins when no other recipe is at least as good on both held-out loss (2 decimals) and rare words guessed (tenths of a percent) and better on one. `WINNERS` (three, all 50% real) is pinned and recomputed in `tests/unit/synthetic-data-engine.test.ts`. At the document level dedupe drops nothing (whole documents never overlap by half), so dedupe and off tie; the table says what each recipe kept.
- **Pinned** (`data/synthetic-data/pinned.json`): chapter 1's mention counts (recounted from the corpus), chapter 2's and 3's batch counts, the teacher's and student's key facts (none of nine). A retrain means new pins, never hand-typed copy.
- **Facts** (`data/synthetic-data/facts.json`): Shumailov et al. (Nature 2024) and Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) are cited as labelled large-model findings, apart from Larkspur's toy; Self-Instruct (2022) and phi-1's "Textbooks Are All You Need" (2023) on the intro.
