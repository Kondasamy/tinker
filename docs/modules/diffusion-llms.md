# Diffusion LMs (`/diffusion-llms/`)

Decisions for this module only; the shared rules are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Picture

- **The type frame** (`src/components/scenes/typeFrameScene.ts`): a printer's iron chase holds the 32-token window as rows of eight slots (just as many rows as the phrase and the reply need, so a shorter reply length shows a smaller frame). The given phrase stands in cream slugs; the reply's slots wait as dark blank slugs ([MASK]); a slug stamped on the pass shown is lit mint, one stamped before is cobalt. Every reply slot carries a mustard glow on its top as wide as the model's confidence in it (`glowOf`: its guess while blank, its stamp once set), so the frame visibly fills in any order, surest first. A press stands behind the frame, its head over the surest slot it just stamped, with that slot's pill (`#n “piece” p%`); chapter 2's blank inside the phrase is a `hole`. Larkspur mini's typewriter stands beside it, its paper growing a line per token typed, and a dial in front points at the tokens stamped a pass (`dialAngle` over `KS`). `FrameStage` (`src/content/diffusion-llms/kit.tsx`) puts it one tab from the Strip (`fillStrip.ts`: a row per pass, a cell per slot, mint stamped this pass, cobalt before, dashed blank, a mustard dot sized by confidence; every cell focusable, arrows across slots and up and down passes, the read-out naming its piece and probability, and the reply after that pass under it). The Strip tab stays off until a fill is done. The new mechanic (KTD15): fills run a pass per animation frame and show a pass every `PASS_MS`, so the frame fills in its own order; with reduced motion only each fill's end shows. On phones the pills keep names and slot numbers, and the caption carries the reply.

## Engine (`src/lib/diffusion-llms/`)

- **Masked decoding runs live; the model was trained once** (KTD2, KTD3). `decode.ts` fills a reply of `L` (16) [MASK] slots after a 16-token held-out phrase with Larkspur's toy masked model (`public/data/diffusion-llms/mlm.bin`, `forward(..., { bidirectional: true })`, the `'mlm'` weights). Each pass reads the whole window, guesses every blank (its argmax, [MASK] ruled out, and that probability) and stamps k of them (`KS`: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16): the surest (confidence remasking, ties to the earlier slot) or k drawn with `mulberry32(seed)` (random remasking). Stamped slots are never masked again; passes are ⌈L / k⌉. It is `train_mlm.py`'s `decode()` pass for pass, and `diffusion-llms-decode.test.ts` writes every pinned reply in `data/diffusion-llms/prompts.json` again (all ten phrases, both rules, every k). The fill's random draws live off the `Fill` (a WeakMap), so a fill is plain data a page can serialise. Larkspur mini's left-to-right baseline is Inference's `greedy`, one pass a token.
- **Reply quality is agreement with the same model's careful fill, plus a repeat rate** (the orchestrator's decision, overriding KTD11's held-out-reference quality: a toy this small almost never writes unseen text token for token). The careful fill is k = 1, confidence remasking, same phrase, L = 16; a reply's quality is the share of its tokens equal to the careful fill's, slot for slot (`agreeing`), and its repeat rate the share of tokens equal to the one before (`repeating`). Measured over the ten pinned phrases: agreement 1.000, .225, .206, .150, .138 at k = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 (random never above confidence for k > 1); repeats rise from .053 to .780 (confidence) and .093 to .780 (random). Chapter 4 ("Faster and worse") teaches the drop with both readouts; chapter 3 compares confidence with random remasking. The copy calls the model Larkspur's toy masked model every time (`ToyNote`).
- **Fills run in slices** (`useFill` in the kit): one pass per animation frame (a pass is a few milliseconds), shown a pass every `PASS_MS`; a fill already run on a page comes back at once. Nothing decodes at import, so the copy-budget test's eager chapter imports stay cheap. Models load once per page through the kit's own store (`useModel('mlm' | 'base')`), with a loading pill or an error card with Retry.
- **Pinned outputs** (KTD12, `data/diffusion-llms/pinned.json`): the workbench's `WINNERS` and chapter 5's fills of one release-note phrase at 4, 8 and 16 slots (each frame filled exactly, its last slot stamped before its first). `diffusion-llms-workbench.test.ts` recomputes both live and fails on drift, with the chapters' claims (chapter 2's real token in the blank's top five, chapter 3's surest-first beating random on its phrase, chapter 4's fastest setting drifting and stuttering, chapter 6's first setting to reach the claim).
- **Chapter 6's arithmetic**: tokens a second = tokens a pass × passes a second. The vendor's figure is its own, dated (fact `mercury-2`, Inception's blog); passes a second is Larkspur's planning number (`data/diffusion-llms/assumptions.json`), labelled as one; `NEED` is the first k that reaches the claim (`chapters.ts`, shared with the e2e walker, which can't import a chapter file).
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): tokens a pass × remasking per kind of reply (customer answers, release-note drafts, internal notes; `data/diffusion-llms/workbench.json`, each kind's pinned phrases and the team's bars: the fewest tokens matching the careful fill and the most repeats). A kind passes when every one of its phrases' pinned replies keeps its bars; cost is passes a reply, summed over the kinds. `WINNERS` is every plan at the fewest passes (two today: notes pass at 8 a pass either way), pinned and recomputed by `solve()` in a test, never at import. The start is the vendor's default (chapter 6's `NEED`, surest first, everywhere), which fails.
- **Facts about diffusion language models** come from `data/diffusion-llms/facts.json` with source and access date: LLaDA (remasking, the preset length), MDLM, SEDD, Fast-dLLM, Gemini Diffusion and Mercury 2's speed, labelled the vendor's own figure.
