# Recommendations (`/recommendations/`)

Decisions for this module only; the shared rules are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Picture

- **The shop aisle** (`src/components/scenes/shelfScene.ts`): at the back, the "you might also need" cabinet with five product boxes, ranked from the left, each in its aisle's colour (Woodwork mustard, Fittings blush, Warehouse cobalt, Metalwork mint); an explored (random) slot's pill reads "?". The slots stand 34 apart along x, so each sits lower on screen than the last and one row of pills never overlaps. In the **aisle view** (chapters 1–3) the floor holds the shopper, up to three similar shoppers and the basket product. In the **map view** (chapters 4–7) the floor is the taste map: every product with a taste is a tile at its two numbers (at most 40; the shelf's five stand taller), an aisle pill over each aisle's middle, the shopper at their own two numbers. A product with no taste (the Kestrel K3 before its first orders) stands off the map on a crate. The map's range is fixed from the pinned tastes, so dots never rescale. `shelfPlot` is ready for the home hero's `DRAW`. `RecsStage` (`src/content/recommendations/kit.tsx`) puts it one tab from the **Ranking** table (the top eight with their scores, the shelf's five marked).
- **The mechanic (KTD13): move a shopper.** In the map view the shopper is a focusable marker (`role="button"`, `aria-roledescription="movable shopper"`) drawn as an IsoStage child over the picture: arrow keys move it a fixed step (6 floor units) only while it has focus, with a solid focus ring; Tab leaves it; a drag moves it (pointer → floor by the inverse projection). The shelf re-ranks at once. A native **Shopper** (or **Basket**) select is the list alternative. Motion (a product sliding onto the shelf, the marker gliding) lives only behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` in `recommendations.css`; with reduced motion the same update lands at once.
- **One read-out.** A single polite `role="status"` under the stage names the shelf, with "Nearest <aisle>" after a move; chapters 6 and 7 pass their round or setting summary instead.
- **The ranking is by direction.** The shelf ranks by dot product, so moving the shopper outward from the origin changes nothing; "nearest aisle" is by angle for the same reason.

## Engine (`src/lib/recommendations/`)

- **Made-up orders** (`data/recommendations/orders.json`, generated once by a seeded script, not real data): 60 invented trade customers, 40 products, one product per order, oldest first. Product names reuse Larkspur's catalogue from other modules where it has them (Tool calling's orders, Databases' and Caching's items, the Kestrel K3 from the RAG corpus). Tastes run along one line, Fittings → Woodwork → Metalwork → Warehouse, each customer's second aisle a neighbour on it, so two factors can carry them. Each customer's last order is new to them and held out; the K3 has no orders.
- **Methods** (`score.ts`, `similar.ts`): bestsellers; user-user and item-item cosine over bought/not bought; tastes. Every shelf skips what the customer owns; ties go to catalogue order. hit@5 counts held-out orders on the shelf; coverage counts products on any shelf.
- **`factorise()`** (`factorise.ts`): implicit-feedback ALS (Hu, Koren and Volinsky), 2 factors, ALPHA 16, LAMBDA 0.1, 15 iterations from a mulberry32 seed (7); the two sides are rebalanced to the same scale (every dot product unchanged) so customers and products share the map. `factors.json` pins the tastes from the training orders and after the K3's first three orders; the engine test recomputes them within 1e-9.
- **The loop** (`loop.ts`): each round every customer sees a shelf (a rank blend of bestsellers and tastes, then `explore` random slots), buys one liked shelf product with a stated 80% chance, and the shop retrains on the last 3 rounds (the made-up orders are round 0). Products off every shelf lose their orders, then their taste, then their place on the map: that count is the loop's coverage. Each round's map is turned (`align`, an orthogonal Procrustes fit) onto round 0's so it doesn't spin; shelves use the tastes as fitted. Every reachable run (5 blends × 3 exploration steps × 7 rounds) is pinned in `loop.json`, one run per line; the browser only replays it.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): the rule (`workbench.json`) keeps at least 30 of 40 products on the map after the loop, then the most hits. `WINNERS` is pinned in `pinned.json` and `recommendations-workbench.test.ts` solves it again from fresh runs. `WRITE_PINNED=1` rewrites every pin.
- **Facts** (Linden, Smith and York 2003; Resnick et al. 1994; Koren, Bell and Volinsky 2009; Hu, Koren and Volinsky 2008; Schein et al. 2002; Chaney, Stewart and Engelhardt 2018; Covington, Adams and Sargin 2016) come from `data/recommendations/facts.json` with source and access date. Chapter 1 links to Search's ranking chapter (R17).

## Toy vs real

Every count below is computed for real in the browser, but over made-up orders (`OrdersNote` on every chapter says so; chapter 6 also names the buying chance a planning number). The cited fact on the same step is the real figure it stands beside. The shop is small on purpose: 60 customers and 40 products so every product fits on the floor, two factors so the taste map is a floor at all. No real figure fits a toy constant without breaking those decisions, so nothing is matched.

| 1 | 60 customers, 40 products, a shelf of 5, the five the two shelves share, bestsellers' hits in 60, products that ever reach a bestsellers shelf | `top-sellers` (Amazon: personal recommendations vastly beat top-seller lists on clicks and sales) | Labelled: made-up orders |
