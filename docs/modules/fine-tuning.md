# Fine-tuning (`/fine-tuning/`)

Decisions for this module only; the shared rules are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Picture

- **The training bench** (`src/components/scenes/trainScene.ts`): the model (RAG's LLM block, composed) at the back, and three lever stations in front, one per way to change an answer: a prompt card, a document binder and a weights wrench, each wired to the model. The chosen lever is pulled and lit with its wire; for LoRA a small mint adapter clips onto the model's side, and the model's pill names the weights it runs (base, fine-tuned, base + adapter). `FineTuneStage` (`src/content/fine-tuning/kit.tsx`) puts it one tab from Results: a loss chart (`lossChart.ts`, flat, with focusable steps and a read-out line) or the Evals module's grid, a column per version. A stage that needs weights holds its frame with a loading pill, or an error card with Retry, until they are in. Chapters 1, 2, 4–7 keep the tab in the step state; on phones only the lit lever keeps its pill.
- **Hero object: the lever frame.** A cast-iron frame of three levers with rubber grips on notched quadrants; on its bed a recipe-card box (prompt), a ring binder (documents) and a real wrench (weights). The model machine stands at the back with the clip-on adapter; a technician stands beside the frame.

## Engine (`src/lib/fine-tuning/`)

- **Larkspur mini's fine-tunes are real; the assistant's answers are recorded** (KTD9). Two halves in `engine.ts`: the fine-tunes, run live in the browser, and three versions of Larkspur's assistant, whose answers are recorded and graded live.
- **Two real fine-tunes, from the committed `model.bin`.** `train.py --finetune` never retrains the base: a full fine-tune (every weight, `public/data/lm/finetuned.bin`, model.bin's layout and size) and LoRA (rank 4, alpha 4, on each layer's `attn.qkv` and `attn.proj`, `public/data/lm/lora.bin`, 3 KB) train on the style set, `data/lm/style-set.jsonl`, encoded into `data/lm/ids.json` `styleSet` by `pnpm encode:lm`. Every reply in it signs off "Cheers, the Larkspur team", which the corpus never says: the style signal (`signsOff`). Each run's loss curve, held-out and style scores go in `meta.json` `finetune` / `lora`, and its goldens in `goldens.json` `finetuned` / `lora`, computed from the Float16-rounded weights like the base's. Base `model.bin` and the base goldens never change: `tests/unit/fine-tuning-runs.test.ts` pins `model.bin`'s sha256.
- **Everything shown about the fine-tunes is computed**: parameter counts from the shapes in `meta.json` (`fullCount`, and `loraCount`, rank × (in + out) per adapted matrix), greedy replies (`replyOf`, so the same weights always write the same words), and held-out loss computed live over the held-out split (`heldoutScore`; `data/fine-tuning/heldout.json` is a copy of that split so chapter 5 doesn't bundle all of `ids.json`, and a test proves the copy matches). Chapter 5 fetches all three weight sets on load, so it plays on offline. The copy says these are Larkspur mini's numbers, not any provider's.
- **Three versions of the assistant** (`data/fine-tuning/versions.json`): prompted (the style in the system prompt), RAG (the same prompt plus Evals' v3 retrieval) and fine-tuned. Their answers are recorded in `data/fine-tuning/answers.json`, keyed by version and question, over Evals' twelve questions and the changed price. They are graded live by Evals' code graders against `data/evals/references.json`, and gated by Evals' `decide`. Evals' data and recordings are read, never edited.
- **The changed-price case keeps its own fixture** (`data/fine-tuning/price-change.json`): a price-update passage the RAG version's recorded answer cites, and a reference with the new price as its key fact. It is graded by the same rule as Evals' key-fact grader through `keyFactOf` in `src/lib/evals/graders.ts`, which takes a reference; `keyFact` calls it, so Evals' grading is unchanged. `data/corpus/`, the RAG index and `data/evals/` stay as they were.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): each kind of request goes to one version, a finite domain of 3^kinds plans. Quality is read off the recorded answers (the key fact, or the sign-off for replies with no facts); cost and upkeep are the team's scoring notes in `data/fine-tuning/workbench.json`, each with its reason. The puzzle accepts every plan tied for the best score (`WINNERS`), not one hand-picked answer.
- **Facts about fine-tuning** come from `data/fine-tuning/facts.json` with source and access date. No fact names a platform as the place to fine-tune; availability facts (OpenAI's self-serve wind-down) carry their dates and are re-checked when the module changes.

## Toy vs real

Nothing here is matched (KTD5): Larkspur mini's fine-tunes were trained ahead of time and the three versions' answers are recorded, so every toy number is labelled. Chapters 2, 3 and 5 show `ToyNote` (a toy, its weight count and style-set size, "the numbers are not" a real model's) beside a cited fact; chapters 1, 4, 6 and 7 show `RecordedNote` (answers recorded, grading live; chapter 6 adds that the 75% bar is hand-set). Numbers in quiz scenarios (500 or 2,000 replies, 30 or 45 days, 9:01) are made-up cases, not measurements.
