# AGENTS.md

Decisions for anyone (human or agent) changing this repo. Each one is here because it was chosen on purpose; change it only on purpose.

## Principles

- Cut taglines, helper text and secondary labels. If a screen needs them to make sense, fix the layout.
- Make the best decision that will elevate all three: the user experience (UX), developer experience (DX), and agent experience (AX) always, without breaking anything.

- **Registry (`src/lib/modules.ts`).** Each module declares itself in `src/content/<module>/module.ts` (id, title, level, status, author, prereqs, story, chapters with `intro`, recall bank); `MODULES` collects them. Pages, navigation, share images and checks read from it. Planned modules are registry entries with no chapters, no pages and no images.
- **Levels are per module**: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced. RAG is Beginner; Tool calling is Intermediate with RAG as its prereq; AI agents is Advanced with Tool calling as its prereq; Evals is Intermediate with RAG as its prereq; Guardrails is Intermediate with Tool calling as its prereq; Tokens is Beginner with no prereqs; Transformers is Advanced with Tokens as its prereq; Context is Intermediate with RAG and Tool calling as its prereqs; Fine-tuning is Advanced with Evals and Transformers as its prereqs; Structured outputs is Intermediate with Tool calling and Tokens as its prereqs; Reasoning is Intermediate with Evals as its prereq; Small models is Advanced with Fine-tuning and Evals as its prereqs; Inference is Advanced with Transformers as its prereq; Observability is Intermediate with Evals as its prereq; Sandboxing is Advanced with Agents and Guardrails as its prereqs; Multimodal is Intermediate with Tokens as its prereq; Voice is Advanced with Tool calling and Multimodal as its prereqs; Pre-training is Intermediate with Tokens as its prereq; Post-training is Advanced with Fine-tuning and Evals as its prereqs; Interpretability is Advanced with Transformers as its prereq; Caching is Beginner with no prereqs; Databases is Beginner with no prereqs; Consistency is Advanced with Databases as its prereq; A/B testing is Beginner with no prereqs; Diffusion models is Intermediate with Multimodal as its prereq.
- **Each module keeps its own page folder**: `src/pages/<module>/index.astro`, `[chapter].astro`, `recap.astro`. The chapter route picks its island with a ternary chain, one line per chapter: hydration needs a static import, so no lookup table and no shared `[module]/[chapter]` route.
- **Progress is per module** in `localStorage` key `<module>-progress-v1` (RAG's is the original `rag-progress-v1`, no migration), plus `tinker-last-module`. Every read tolerates missing fields and missing storage.
- **Never rename a module id or chapter id**: saved progress is keyed by them.
- **Saved runs carry their step ids.** A run resumes only if its saved ids match the chapter's current ones; otherwise the chapter restarts (`done` is never touched). RAG chapters keep a `legacySteps` signature for runs saved before signing. Changing a chapter's steps restarts its in-progress runs; that is intended.

## Making it stick

- **Predict → reveal:** before a reveal the reader commits a pick (`Predict`); it locks and stays beside the outcome.
- **Quizzes explain every option** (`why` per option) and offer "I'm not sure", which reveals without counting as a miss.
- **Recall bank** per module (`src/content/<module>/recall.ts`), one item per bank pinned (`pin`) so it comes first until answered right. Results live in progress, outside the run, so Reset never wipes them.
- **Recap** draws from the bank for finished chapters with a stored seed (a reload keeps the order). **Warm-up:** up to three questions when the reader returns 20 h or more after finishing a chapter, on the module intro and through home's Continue, with a skip link.
- Nothing a reader enters in a chapter leaves the browser. The home page's request form is the one exception, and only on submit.

Marketing pages share `src/styles/page.css` (buttons, kickers, chips, top bar, credits). Chapter pages use `src/layouts/ChapterLayout.astro`.

**CSS:** layout-level rules that style elements inside components (e.g. sizing every `svg` in the stage) go in `:where(...)` so component rules always win. Same-specificity rules otherwise depend on the order Astro bundles the CSS, which changes as pages are added (this once made the chapter 6 graph walk overlap a block). Dev-only pages use scoped styles, never `is:global`: dev pages are bundled into `DEV_PAGES` builds, and global rules leak into real pages.

## Chapter page layout

- **Wide screens (≥ 861px): an app frame.** The picture holds still on the left; the story column on the right scrolls on its own. Phones stack the picture above the story.
- **The chapter bar has two fixed rows** (brand and credit, then the chapter nav, which scrolls sideways instead of wrapping), so it is the same height on every chapter. Progress marks keep a fixed slot. Moving between chapters cross-fades (`@view-transition`, off with reduced motion).
- **Every visit opens on the chapter's intro**, a resumed or finished run too; the intro then offers "Continue at step N". The story column scrolls to the step only when the step changes.
- **The story column opens with context, kept short** (picture-first chapters): before step 1 come the chapter number, title, the story so far and the chapter's question; "You will learn" and "Words you'll meet" fold under a closed "About this chapter". This lives in each chapter's `intro` in `src/content/<module>/module.ts`. Never drop a reader straight into a step, and never put a wall of text before it: testers called the old opening (about 250 words) too text-heavy.
- **Chapter words are defined where they are first used**: `StepRunner` marks each of the chapter's `terms` at its first use in the narration (`src/components/story/terms.ts`), a button that opens the definition beside the step card, outside the step's live region.
- **Finished steps stay reachable** as a ticked "story so far" log folded to a count ("Story so far (n)") above the current step card; the current step scrolls into view when it changes.
- **What to do goes in `act`**, not at the end of the narration: the runner draws it as its own bold, highlighted line under the narration (testers could not tell "Click 2020." from the text around it). Chapter words in it are marked too. Every module uses it on every step that asks for something.
- **Copy budgets** (`tests/unit/copy-budget.test.ts`): a step's narration and its `act` together are at most 25 words, the story 30, the question 20, "You will learn" 3 items, a callout 5 words, each quiz `why` one sentence, story + question + step 1 together 62 (so the first screen stays at or under 80 words with its labels), and every chapter term appears in the narration or the `act`. Every module is strict (`STRICT=<id>` was the per-module to-do list during the copy pass). An e2e check counts the first screen of every RAG chapter and of each module's chapter 1.
- Steps are run by `StepRunner` (`src/components/story/`): narration, the one control the step needs, a predicate that advances it, hints after 3 misses, "Show me" for puzzles, progress in `localStorage`.

## Story and content rules

- **One story per module.** RAG: you join the team building an AI assistant for **Larkspur Supply Co.**, a fictional hardware company. (An early "noir detective" concept was dropped.)
- **Every fact comes from the data.** History numbers come from `data/history/timeline.json` (with sources). Answers are the recorded ones in `data/answers/`. Counts come from the built index (`public/data/index/manifest.json`). Do not type numbers or answers into page copy by hand; import them.
- Plain words, short sentences, one idea per step. Define a term the first time it appears (and add it to the chapter's `terms`). Let the picture show what happened; the narration says what it means and what to do next.
- **Sources open from a mark** (`src/components/story/Cite.tsx`): the claim stays in the text, a small ↗ mark opens its source title, link and checked date in a popover. Every module's `Fact` and RAG's `Source` render through it; no inline "Source:" links in copy.

## Tool calling engine (`src/lib/tools/`)

- **Recorded model turns, live everything else.** Turns live in `data/tool-calling/turns/*.json`, keyed by a signature of what the model could see; a missing one is `norecord`. Every chapter control has a finite exported domain, and a unit test generates every reachable signature from them and asserts each has a turn. Host-only decisions (call cap, permissions, validation) stay out of the signature.
- **Schema checks are a hand-written JSON Schema subset** (`schema.ts`); copy says "a subset". No `ajv` (it compiles with `new Function`).
- **The MCP server is a model of the protocol**, not a conforming SDK: JSON-RPC 2.0 shaped, following the current spec (stateless, 2026-07-28, no opening handshake). Every MCP or API fact in copy comes from `data/tool-calling/facts.json` with its source and access date.

## Agent engine (`src/lib/agents/`)

- **Its own folder, built on `src/lib/tools/`.** It reuses the schema checks, the MCP-shaped server, the turn lookup and `host.ts`'s `combosOf`. It has its own loop and signature, so Tool calling's signatures and recordings never change.
- **`server.ts` takes a bundle** (servers, tools, a handler per tool). Tool calling passes nothing and gets its own default. Agents passes `data/agents/server.json`: the order server plus warehouse, carrier and mail, with fixtures in `data/agents/cases.json`.
- **Recorded turns, keyed by what the model saw.** Turns live in `data/agents/turns/<scenario>.json`, one reviewed file per scenario. The signature holds the case, the tools, the plan state, the memory mode and the visible history (each result so far, or the summary's marker after compaction). Host-only settings stay out: the step budget, the repeat detector's setting, the approval mode and the context budget. They change where a run stops, not what the model says. Every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, and `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS` for chapter 7), and `tests/unit/agent-coverage.test.ts` runs every combination and fails on any `norecord`.
- **Tokens are computed, never recorded**: bytes ÷ `BYTES_PER_TOKEN`, the full context re-sent each turn.
- **The repeat detector is a host rule.** When the same call fails the same way N times in a row, the host returns a "stopped" result. That result enters the history, so the model gets one last recorded turn to tell the customer, and the run ends `stuck`. The step budget ends a run with no further turn (`out-of-budget`).
- **Plan approval binds the run.** A call outside the approved plan, or a new plan, goes back to a person. A plan nobody has answered leaves the run `waiting`.
- **The chapter 5 context budget is a host setting** Larkspur chose, not a fact about any model; the copy says so.
- **A sub-agent is a nested run** with its own history and budget; its turns are the `order-<id>` cases in the same scenario recording. The lead gets only the helper's final line, as the result of the host-defined `delegate` tool, so the helper's history never enters the lead's signature.

## Evals engine (`src/lib/evals/`)

- **Versions over RAG's questions, reading recorded answers.** `suite.ts` defines four versions of Larkspur's assistant (v0 no documents, v1 RAG's defaults, v2 bigger pieces, v3 reranker and top 5) as RAG knobs. Running a version is RAG's real retrieval over the committed index on the 12 questions of RAG chapters 3–6, then the recorded answer for what it found (`data/answers/`). No new model answers; a missing one throws.
- **Code graders are computed; everything else is recorded.** Exact match, key fact and citation run live (`graders.ts`) against `data/evals/references.json`. `exactMatchOf(reference, text)` and `keyFactOf` take a reference, so other modules grade their own fixtures by Evals' rules; `exactMatch` and `keyFact` call them. The model judge (`judge.json`: per answer and rubric, keyed by question and signature), the people's labels (`labels.json`) and the repeated tries (`samples.json`, standing in for sampling a live model) are recorded data.
- **Side-by-side judging is recorded in both orders** (`ab`, `ba`); a pair whose winner changes with the order is position bias.
- **The path chapter reads Tool calling's runs**: it plays `order-status` and `injection` through `src/lib/tools/host.ts` and grades the reply, the ledger and the calls. Tool calling's recordings are never edited; the judge's verdicts on those runs live in `judge.json` `runs`.
- **The release gate is a host rule** (`decide`): a pass-rate bar, and optionally no question that passed on the baseline (v1) may fail.
- **Coverage:** every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, and `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS` for chapter 7). `tests/unit/evals-coverage.test.ts` and `evals-workbench.test.ts` walk every combination and fail on any missing answer, reference or judge verdict.
- **Facts about evals** come from `data/evals/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Guardrails engine (`src/lib/guardrails/`)

- **A small single-agent loop with defence layers as host stages** (`loop.ts`). It reuses Tool calling's schema checks, `server.ts`'s `Bundle`, `combosOf` and `stepsOf`/`answerOf`; no plans, compaction or delegation. Input stages (the classifier, the input policy check) decide what the model sees; the taint rule and the trifecta's legs decide which tools it is offered; output stages (approval, the allow-list, the output policy check, the output filter) act on what it does.
- **The bundle** (`data/guardrails/server.json`) adds `read_inbox` (untrusted email), `get_customer` (private data: address, card's last four, account notes) and `send_email` with a free `to` field (the way out) to the order tools. Replies go through the host-defined `reply` tool, which can only answer the sender of the email being worked, so it is not a way out. The taint rule removes only `send_email`.
- **The signature holds what the model could see** (`turns.ts`): the case, the email's form (`full`, `quarantined` when the classifier held it, `refused` when the input policy check turned it away), whether the prompt rule is in the system text, the tools offered (the taint rule and a removed leg change them) and the history of outcomes, a stopped send entering as `send_email:blocked` or `send_email:denied`. Host-only settings stay out: the threshold's value, the allow-list, the output filter, the output policy check and approval. They change what the host does, not what the model sees.
- **The classifier is a fixture, not a model.** Each email in `data/guardrails/inbox.json` carries a hand-set score, an `attack` label and a note; it is the only home of scores and labels. Copy calls it a stand-in detector whose scores were set to show the trade-off. Thresholds come from a finite list (`THRESHOLDS`).
- **Two suites**: `data/guardrails/suite.json` references inbox emails by id, the attacks (each with a goal check) and the ordinary customers (each with the tools its answer needs). `metrics()` runs both under one defence set and gives attack success rate (`asr`) and false-block rate (`fbr`); the copy names the two counts an eval.
- **One merged recording.** Turns live in `data/guardrails/turns/<chapter>.json`, one reviewed file per chapter scenario holding the turns that chapter first needs; each signature is in exactly one file, and every run looks up the merged `RECORDING`, so later chapters and the red-team workbench reuse earlier turns. This departs on purpose from the plan's per-chapter recordings (as agents keeps): the chapters share most of what the model sees, and one lookup keeps a turn from being recorded twice. Chapter 7 has no file of its own.
- **Coverage:** every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, one per recording file) and chapter 7's red team is one domain over every defence and both suites (`WORKBENCH_DOMAINS`). `tests/unit/guard-coverage.test.ts` runs every combination and fails on any `norecord`, on a signature in two files, or on a recorded turn nothing reaches.
- **Chapter 5's puzzle accepts every minimal winning set**: every defence set the controls can make that stops every attack with the fewest false blocks (`WINNERS`, computed over the chapter's domain), not one hand-picked answer.
- **Facts about prompt injection** come from `data/guardrails/facts.json` with source and access date.
- **Agents chapter 3 links straight to `/guardrails/`** for prompt injection; it changed in the PR that took Guardrails live.

## Tokens engine (`src/lib/tokens/`)

- **The site tokenizer runs live; real tokenizers are recorded.** Counts and tiles come from the language-model kit's BPE (`src/lib/lm/bpe.ts`), run in the browser on whatever the reader types. GPT-2 and GPT-4o (o200k_base) are recorded once by `pnpm record:tokens` (`scripts/record-tokenizers.mjs`, `@huggingface/transformers`, models cached in `.cache/models`) into `data/tokens/encodings.json`: ids and pieces for every sentence in `data/tokens/sentences.json`, and token counts for every corpus document. The site never ships their vocabularies. Re-record after editing `sentences.json` or `data/corpus/`; the output is deterministic, so an unchanged input gives an unchanged file.
- **The site tokenizer's corpus count is in-sample** (it was trained on those documents); the copy says so.
- **Neighbours read RAG's committed vectors, read-only** (`neighbours.ts`): cosine over RAG's int8 chunk and question vectors, loaded through RAG's loader for its default chunks. Nothing is re-embedded and RAG's index never changes.
- **RAG's embedding model reads WordPiece, not BPE**: chapter 5 says so (fact `minilm`), so no one takes the site's BPE tokens for what the embedding model sees.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`) builds a prompt from fixed parts and fits it to a finite list of budgets (`BUDGETS`); every prompt that fits with nothing more to add is a right answer.
- **Facts about tokens and embeddings** come from `data/tokens/facts.json` with source and access date; provider facts (prices, tokenizer changes) are re-checked when a module goes live.

## Transformers engine (`src/lib/transformers/`)

- **Nothing is recorded; Larkspur mini runs live.** `trace.ts` runs one forward pass of the language-model kit's model and reads it as the chapters show it: the pieces read (the last 32), the top 5 next tokens at every position and every attention matrix. Build-time pictures (the home card, the intro, share images) read the same weights from disk (`diskModel.ts`, Node only).
- **The sampler is seeded** (`sample.ts`): temperature and top-p over the live logits, drawn with `mulberry32`, so a seed always gives the same continuation (chapter 5 keeps it across a reload, and e2e is deterministic). Temperature and top-p step over finite lists (`TEMPERATURES`, `TOP_PS`).
- **Costs are counted, not measured** (`costs.ts`), from the model's shape: attention pairs per head under the causal mask, n(n + 1) / 2, and the numbers the KV cache keeps, layers × 2 × d_model × n.
- **The readable bar** (`data/transformers/prompts.json`): every prompt a chapter shows has its expected next token in Larkspur mini's top 5; `tests/unit/transformers-engine.test.ts` fails otherwise. A retrain that breaks one means new prompts, not new copy typed by hand.
- **Chapter 4's word-start head is layer 0, head 0**, recorded in `prompts.json` with its measured score (`wordStart()` over every 32-token window of the training and held-out documents: 0.39 against 0.09 for spreading evenly). The test recomputes the score and fails if it drifts or the head stops standing out (more than 3× uniform).
- **Facts about transformers** come from `data/transformers/facts.json` with source and access date; the papers, Anthropic's context-engineering note and the nanoGPT, 3Blue1Brown and Transformer Explainer pages are re-checked when the module goes live.

## Context engine (`src/lib/context/`)

- **A turn assembler plus recorded answers keyed by what the model saw** (`loop.ts`, `window.ts`), mirroring Guardrails' turns. The host builds each window from parts: the system prompt, the tools, the history (`full`, `cleared` when old tool results are dropped, `summary`), the retrieved documents (which, in what order, where the needed fact sits) and memory notes (none or read). Token counts come from the language-model kit's BPE, live; the copy says they are this site's counts, not a provider's.
- **The signature is what the model sees** (`turns.ts`): the case, the system prompt's form, the tools offered, whether every document is stuffed, the documents and search results in window order, the history and the memory files read. Host-only settings stay out: the token budget, the cache breakpoints, the cache lifetime, the gap between calls and the prices. They change cost or where a run stops, not what the model says.
- **Over budget is refused.** A window whose tokens plus the reply's room (`REPLY_ROOM`) pass the budget gets no model turn (`over-budget`, the refused window kept for the picture), so nothing needs recording and the budget stays host-only.
- **A cache hit is the longest live entry whose prefix still matches, up to its last breakpoint at or before the first difference** (`cache.ts`). The host keeps the session's live entries (each call's parts and breakpoints, plus the prefix it read, which the read refreshes); the call then writes everything after the hit up to its own last breakpoint. A later session starts cold, and an entry neither written nor read within the lifetime expires. No minimum cacheable length and no lookback limit (`shortcut:` comments say so; Larkspur's windows are small on purpose), and chapter 4 shows the minimum as a fact.
- **Costs are in relative units, never dollars**: a fresh input token is 1, a cache read, a cache write (5-minute or 1-hour) and an output token are the dated multipliers in `data/context/facts.json`. The copy says so.
- **One merged recording.** Turns live in `data/context/turns/<chapter>.json`, one reviewed file per chapter holding the turns that chapter first needs; a signature is in exactly one file, and every run looks up the merged `RECORDING`. Every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, one per file) and chapter 7's policy bench is `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS`. `tests/unit/context-coverage.test.ts` runs every combination and fails on any `norecord`, on a signature in two files, on a recorded turn nothing reaches, or past the budget of 150 turns.
- **Answers illustrate sourced findings, they are not measured** (KTD8): each recorded turn carries a one-line `note` and a verdict, and chapter 2's answers by position and distractor count follow Liu et al. (2023) and Chroma (2025), cited in the reveal.
- **Memory is files the host keeps** (`memory.ts`): a note the model writes with the memory tool persists across sessions and is read at the next session's start when the tool is on, the only thing that crosses from one conversation to the next.
- **Chapter 4's and chapter 7's puzzles accept every winning answer**: every block order that reaches the best cache hit rate, and every policy with the most right answers at the lowest cost.
- **Facts about context windows, caching and memory** come from `data/context/facts.json` with source and access date; caching multipliers, lifetimes and window sizes move the most and are re-checked when the module changes.

## Fine-tuning engine (`src/lib/fine-tuning/`)

- **Larkspur mini's fine-tunes are real; the assistant's answers are recorded** (KTD9). Two halves in `engine.ts`: the fine-tunes, run live in the browser, and three versions of Larkspur's assistant, whose answers are recorded and graded live.
- **Two real fine-tunes, from the committed `model.bin`.** `train.py --finetune` never retrains the base: a full fine-tune (every weight, `public/data/lm/finetuned.bin`, model.bin's layout and size) and LoRA (rank 4, alpha 4, on each layer's `attn.qkv` and `attn.proj`, `public/data/lm/lora.bin`, 3 KB) train on the style set, `data/lm/style-set.jsonl`, encoded into `data/lm/ids.json` `styleSet` by `pnpm encode:lm`. Every reply in it signs off "Cheers, the Larkspur team", which the corpus never says: the style signal (`signsOff`). Each run's loss curve, held-out and style scores go in `meta.json` `finetune` / `lora`, and its goldens in `goldens.json` `finetuned` / `lora`, computed from the Float16-rounded weights like the base's. Base `model.bin` and the base goldens never change: `tests/unit/fine-tuning-runs.test.ts` pins `model.bin`'s sha256.
- **Everything shown about the fine-tunes is computed**: parameter counts from the shapes in `meta.json` (`fullCount`, and `loraCount`, rank × (in + out) per adapted matrix), greedy replies (`replyOf`, so the same weights always write the same words), and held-out loss computed live over the held-out split (`heldoutScore`; `data/fine-tuning/heldout.json` is a copy of that split so chapter 5 doesn't bundle all of `ids.json`, and a test proves the copy matches). Chapter 5 fetches all three weight sets on load, so it plays on offline. The copy says these are Larkspur mini's numbers, not any provider's.
- **Three versions of the assistant** (`data/fine-tuning/versions.json`): prompted (the style in the system prompt), RAG (the same prompt plus Evals' v3 retrieval) and fine-tuned. Their answers are recorded in `data/fine-tuning/answers.json`, keyed by version and question, over Evals' twelve questions and the changed price. They are graded live by Evals' code graders against `data/evals/references.json`, and gated by Evals' `decide`. Evals' data and recordings are read, never edited.
- **The changed-price case keeps its own fixture** (`data/fine-tuning/price-change.json`): a price-update passage the RAG version's recorded answer cites, and a reference with the new price as its key fact. It is graded by the same rule as Evals' key-fact grader through `keyFactOf` in `src/lib/evals/graders.ts`, which takes a reference; `keyFact` calls it, so Evals' grading is unchanged. `data/corpus/`, the RAG index and `data/evals/` stay as they were.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): each kind of request goes to one version, a finite domain of 3^kinds plans. Quality is read off the recorded answers (the key fact, or the sign-off for replies with no facts); cost and upkeep are the team's scoring notes in `data/fine-tuning/workbench.json`, each with its reason. The puzzle accepts every plan tied for the best score (`WINNERS`), not one hand-picked answer.
- **Facts about fine-tuning** come from `data/fine-tuning/facts.json` with source and access date. No fact names a platform as the place to fine-tune; availability facts (OpenAI's self-serve wind-down) carry their dates and are re-checked when the module changes.

## Structured outputs engine (`src/lib/structured-outputs/`)

- **Nothing is recorded; Larkspur mini decodes live under a mask** (`decode.ts`). Tool calling's schema subset is compiled to a state machine over bytes (`grammar.ts`): every property required, keys in declared order, no whitespace, values an enum, a boolean, an integer or a capped string (printable ASCII, no escapes). At each step a token is allowed when every one of its bytes keeps the grammar live (`mask.ts`, no precomputed index: 768 tokens is cheap), and the blocked logits go to -Infinity before softmax, so the allowed tokens share the probability. Greedy by default; chapter 5 draws at temperature 1 with `mulberry32` seeds.
- **The 32-token rule.** Prompt and answer share Larkspur mini's 32-token window: a run ends `done` when the grammar accepts, or `cut-off` when the window runs out first. Chapter 6's two-field schema is cut off on purpose; chapter 7's all-model schema is too.
- **`maxLength` is this site's extension** to the schema subset (`FieldSchema`), so a free string always closes inside the window; a string with none gets `DEFAULT_MAX_LENGTH`. Anthropic's structured outputs reject it (fact `anthropic-limits`), and chapter 4 says so.
- **The readable bar** (`data/structured-outputs/prompts.json`): every run a chapter shows is pinned with its prompt, schema, seed and the output Larkspur mini writes; `tests/unit/structured-decode.test.ts` reruns every one and fails on drift. A retrain that breaks one means a new prompt, not new copy.
- **The model ignores the prompt once it is writing JSON.** Larkspur mini never saw JSON, so inside a schema it writes what it prefers, not what the prompt says (given order 1042, it writes another number). The lessons are about preference and what the mask can force, never about what the model knows: facts come from the order system (`data/structured-outputs/orders.json`), as chapter 7 teaches.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): each field is free text, a list (or a whole number), or left to the order system, a finite domain of 27 schemas decoded over four orders. Every schema that gives valid, right answers for every order is a right answer (`WINNERS`). Decoding all 27 froze the page for seconds, so the chapter reads `SOLVED`, `WINNERS` precomputed on the committed weights; a unit test keeps the two equal, and a retrain changes both.
- **Facts about structured outputs** come from `data/structured-outputs/facts.json` with source and access date; provider limits and features are re-checked when the module changes.


## Reasoning engine (`src/lib/reasoning/`)

- **Recorded answers, everything else live** (`engine.ts`). Each question × effort has a recorded thinking and answer, each with a one-line note (`data/reasoning/answers.json`); a missing one throws. The token counts (the language-model kit's BPE), the grade, the cost and the wait are computed when the page runs.
- **Effort levels, not token budgets**: `EFFORTS` is none, low, medium and high, naming the APIs' effort settings; chapter 5 says today's APIs take effort and adaptive thinking, not a fixed budget (facts `effort-levels`, `adaptive`, `budget-deprecated`).
- **Three kinds of question** (`data/reasoning/questions.json`): policy lookups (Evals' own questions, in Evals' words), multi-step quotes, stock and delivery questions, and simple counts wrapped in distractions. Lookups are graded by Evals' `keyFact` against Evals' references; the rest by `keyFactOf` against `data/reasoning/references.json`. Evals' data is only read.
- **Thinking is counted and billed as output**: cost is thinking plus answer tokens, in relative units (one output token is 1), never dollars. The wait comes from Larkspur's assumed rate in `data/reasoning/assumptions.json` (a first token after half a second, then a fixed rate), an assumption, not a provider figure; every chapter that shows a wait says so.
- **The answers illustrate sourced findings, they are not measured** (KTD8): how much thinking helps depending on the question follows Snell et al. (2024), and overthinking on simple questions with distractions follows Gema et al. (2025), cited in the reveal.
- **Chapter 6's gate is Evals' `decide`**: a pass-rate bar, and no question that was right with no thinking may go wrong (`GATE`, baseline `none`); the right setting per kind is the lowest effort that ships (`lowestPassing`).
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): an effort per kind of request, a finite domain of 4^kinds plans, read off the recorded answers. Every plan with the most right answers at the lowest cost wins (`WINNERS`), not one hand-picked answer.
- **Coverage:** every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, and `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS` for chapter 7). `tests/unit/reasoning-coverage.test.ts` walks every combination and fails on any missing answer, on a recorded answer nothing reaches, or past the budget of 120 answers.
- **Facts about reasoning models** come from `data/reasoning/facts.json` with source and access date; effort settings and billing move fast and are re-checked when the module changes.

## Small models engine (`src/lib/small-models/`)

- **Two halves** (KTD6): the teacher and its student, run live (chapters 1–3, `compare.ts`), and two recorded tiers of Larkspur's assistant, routed (chapters 4–7, `router.ts`).
- **The student runs live against the base** (the language-model kit's student, below). Sizes and work per token are counted from the shapes in `meta.json`; the top 5 (`top5`, `softTop5` at a temperature), the agreement (held-out positions where the student's greedy next token is the base's) and the KL divergence come from forward passes over Fine-tuning's held-out copy (`data/fine-tuning/heldout.json`, read only). Nothing in this half is recorded. The models load through Fine-tuning's loaders (`loadModel`, `modelNow`, `ModelGate`), so a page loads each weight set once; chapters 1 and 3 fetch both on load, so they play on offline.
- **Two tiers, recorded** (`data/small-models/answers.json`): a small and a large answer per question over Evals' twelve questions, each with a one-line note; a missing one throws. They are graded live by Evals' `keyFact` against `data/evals/references.json`, and gated by Evals' `decide` (`GATE`: a 90% bar, and no question all-large gets right may fail; `cheapestShipping` is the cheapest threshold that ships). Evals' data is only read.
- **Confidence is a fixture, not a model.** Each small answer carries a hand-set confidence, a stand-in score set to show the trade-off, as Guardrails' classifier scores are; the copy says so. The router sends an answer up to the large tier when its confidence is below the threshold, and thresholds come from a finite list (`THRESHOLDS`). The frontier illustrates what the cascade and router papers report (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, cited in the reveal); it was written for this site, not measured.
- **Costs are in relative units, never dollars**: a large answer is 1, a small one the dated price ratio of a small and a large model from one vendor (fact `price-pair`, Claude Haiku 4.5 against Claude Opus 4.5). A routed answer that goes up pays for both.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): each kind of question goes to the small tier, the large tier or the router, with one threshold, a finite domain of 3^kinds × thresholds plans. A kind scores the team's points per right answer (`data/small-models/workbench.json`, with its reason) minus its cost. Every plan tied for the best score wins (`WINNERS`), not one hand-picked answer.
- **Coverage:** every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, and `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS` for chapter 7). `tests/unit/small-models-coverage.test.ts` walks every combination and fails on any missing answer, on an answer recorded twice or nothing reaches, on a small answer without a confidence in [0, 1], or past the budget of 30 answers.
- **Facts about distillation, cascades, routers and prices** come from `data/small-models/facts.json` with source and access date; the price pair moves the most and is re-checked when the module changes.

## Inference engine (`src/lib/inference/`)

- **Three parts, nothing recorded** (KTD7): quantization and speculative decoding run Larkspur mini (and its student) live in the browser; batching and the KV cache at scale run on a simulated server.
- **Quantization is simulated** (`quantize.ts`): every weight matrix is rounded to int8 or int4 per row (absmax: each row's largest magnitude maps to the top level), then dequantized, and the forward pass runs unchanged. No new weight files. Memory is counted from the shapes, and counts what a real weight-only scheme keeps: one 16-bit scale per row, and the vectors (biases, LayerNorm) at 16 bits. Held-out loss and top-5 changes are computed live, a document at a time so the page keeps responding.
- **Speculative decoding is real** (`speculate.ts`): the student (the language-model kit's, below) drafts k tokens (`KS`), Larkspur mini checks them in one pass and keeps the longest prefix it agrees with plus its own next token. Every run is capped to the 32-token window (`maxNew`), so the checking pass sees what plain greedy decoding saw, and `tests/unit/inference-speculate.test.ts` proves the output equal to greedy, token for token.
- **The server is a discrete-time simulator** (`server.ts`) over a fixed trace of Monday-morning requests (`data/inference/requests.json`, hand-written, not logged traffic). Each iteration's length comes from counted work (parameter counts and Transformers' `costs.ts`) at the round unit costs in `data/inference/assumptions.json`, labelled a cost model: never measured, and the copy says so. Settings step over finite lists: static or continuous batching (`MODES`), the batch cap (`CAPS`) and the KV room (`MEMORY`).
- **Scale comes from a published config**: the server runs OPT-13B's shape (fact `opt-13b`, layers, heads, d_model and parameters), so the KV cache per token and the memory it fills are real-sized; Larkspur mini is far too small to fill a server.
- **Chapter 6 teaches pace against throughput**: on the simulator a bigger batch cap always shortens the queue, so the real cost of a bigger batch is each customer's time per token. The chapter was renamed for it (`06-pace-or-throughput`); keep the framing.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a policy is a mode, a cap and a KV room (2 × 5 × 3). A request passes when its first token comes within Larkspur's first-token target and its reply keeps the per-token target (`data/inference/workbench.json`, the team's choices). The puzzle accepts every policy tied for the most requests on target (`WINNERS`).
- **Facts about serving** (prefill and decode, the KV cache, PagedAttention, continuous batching, GPTQ and AWQ, speculative decoding) come from `data/inference/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Observability engine (`src/lib/observability/`)

- **A hand-written day of traffic, nothing measured** (KTD8). `data/observability/traces.json` holds 40 traces of Larkspur's assistant, 18 of them failed: each a span tree (an agent span over retrieval, model calls, tool calls and the reply) shaped like OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions. The copy says "shaped like". Spans carry only attribute names checked against the spec (`gen_ai.operation.name`, `gen_ai.tool.name`; facts `otel-genai`, `otel-operations`). Timings and failures are written by hand, and `problems()` validates every trace in the engine test.

- **Notes are the reader's own and never graded** (`coding.ts`): open coding keeps a free note per failed trace in the chapter's saved run (localStorage, this module's progress, so Reset clears them), capped at `MAX_NOTE` (140), rendered as text only, never sent anywhere. Chapter 3 needs five notes (`NEEDED_NOTES`); a trace without a note carries its suggested note into chapter 4.
- **A grouping is checked against the reference modes whatever the groups are called** (`groupingMatches`): it passes when traces share a group exactly when they share a mode. Counts and the harm-weighted ranking (`fixFirst`, ties kept) are computed from the labels, never typed.
- **Chapter 6 grades eval cases with Evals' own code graders**: a failed trace becomes a case (`cases.ts`), its current reply against the fixed version's recorded answer (`data/observability/fixed.json`), graded live by `keyFactOf` and `hasCitation` from `src/lib/evals/graders.ts`. Evals' data and recordings are read, never edited.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`) triages day 2's thumbs-down queue (`data/observability/day2.json`): a mode per trace and the one fix to ship. The tally counts the reader's labels, but the harm a fix removes is counted from the reference modes, so a mislabelled queue can point at the wrong fix. The puzzle accepts every fix tied for the most harm removed (`WINNERS`).
- **Facts about error analysis and tracing** come from `data/observability/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Sandboxing engine (`src/lib/sandboxing/`)

- **A modelled sandbox; no real code runs** (KTD9). `sandbox.ts` is a policy and `exec(policy, command)`, a pure function: mounts with read-only or writable modes (`ro`, `rw`; the longest matching prefix wins), hidden paths, network egress (`off`, an allow-list through a proxy, or `open`) and the secret either in the sandbox's env (a command can read it) or held by the proxy, which adds it to allowed requests and never shows it to the agent. Three file presets (`PRESETS`: `open`, `scoped`, `nodata`, the last with the private data not mounted). The files and their contents are fixtures in `data/sandboxing/files.json`; the token is a stand-in, never a real key. Nothing reaches the disk or the network.
- **The loop is forked from Guardrails'** (`loop.ts`), not a wider tools `Bundle`: one agent, four tools (`run`, `read_file`, `write_file`, `fetch`, in `data/sandboxing/server.json`), the sandbox a host stage around each call. A call the filesystem stops is `denied`, one the egress stops `blocked`; both enter the history and are stamped REFUSED.
- **The signature is what the agent could see** (`turns.ts`): the case, the tools and the history of outcomes (each `tool[:path|:host]:<outcome>`). Policy reaches it only through those outcomes. Host-only settings stay out: the approval mode (a person asked about a blocked fetch) changes what the host does, not what the agent is shown.
- **One merged recording.** Turns live in `data/sandboxing/turns/<chapter>.json` (27 in all), one reviewed file per chapter scenario holding the turns that chapter first needs; a signature is in exactly one file and every run looks up the merged `RECORDING`. Every chapter control has a finite domain (`DOMAINS`, and `WORKBENCH_DOMAINS` for chapter 7's red team), and `tests/unit/sandboxing-coverage.test.ts` runs every combination and fails on any `norecord`, on a signature in two files, on a recorded turn nothing reaches, or past the budget.
- **Chapters 3 and 6 keep the secret in the env**, so the exfiltration attack reads it and reaches the fetch the network stage acts on; with the proxy holding it the attack would stop earlier and the network door would have nothing to show.
- **One suite, two counts** (`data/sandboxing/suite.json`): four attacks (exfiltration, a hidden key read, a system file written, a record smuggled through the allowed host), each with a goal checked against the run's ledger, and two ordinary tasks, each with the tools it needs. `metrics()` gives attack success rate (`asr`) and false-block rate (`fbr`).
- **Chapter 7's puzzle accepts every minimal winning policy** (`workbench.ts` `WINNERS`): every policy the controls can make that stops every attack with the fewest blocked tasks, not one hand-picked answer.
- **Facts about sandboxing** come from `data/sandboxing/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Voice engine (`src/lib/voice/`)

## Post-training engine (`src/lib/post-training/`)

- **Recorded answers, live toy-policy maths** (KTD4). `data/post-training/candidates.json` holds 4–5 hand-written answers per question (four of Evals' checkable questions, plus a tone question), each with a note, a hand-set starting probability (`ref`) and flags (`best`, `gaming`, `base`). The policy is a categorical distribution over one question's answers; every chapter's copy calls it a toy policy (`TOY`).
- **Graders are rule sets** (`graders.ts`): an answer's reward is how many rules it passes. `fact` and `exact` are Evals' `keyFactOf` and `exactMatchOf` against `data/post-training/references.json`; `number`, `signoff` (Fine-tuning's `signsOff`) and `short` are weak surface checks. `FULL` is the key fact; `WEAK` is a number plus the sign-off, which every gaming answer passes while failing the key fact. Evals' and Fine-tuning's code is only read.
- **DPO is the closed form** (`policy.ts`): a Bradley–Terry reward fitted with an L2 pull to the reader's pairs, then π ∝ π_ref·exp(r/β), β from `BETAS`. Iterating DPO's loss on a table-shaped policy ignores β; the maths fold-away says so and shows `dpoLoss`.
- **GRPO is seeded** (`grpoRun`, `mulberry32`, `seedOf(question)`): a group of `GROUP` drafts, advantage (r − mean) / std, zero for a group with no spread, `INNER` clipped policy-gradient steps (`CLIP`) per round, `ROUNDS` rounds. No KL term (`shortcut:`).
- **Chapter 1 runs Fine-tuning's live `replyOf`** from the base and fine-tuned weights; it links `/fine-tuning/`.
- **Chapter 3 reads chapter 2's saved picks** (`loadChapter`), and uses the team's picks (`TEAM_PICKS`), saying so, when there are none.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): every subset of `RULES` (2^5) run with GRPO on each checkable question; `WINNERS` is every rule set where each question's best answer ends most likely, so no gaming answer wins (20 of 32 today). Tests: `post-training-policy`, `post-training-coverage` (budget 30 answers), `post-training-workbench`, `exam-scene`.
- **Facts about post-training** come from `data/post-training/facts.json` with source and access date: InstructGPT, DPO, DeepSeekMath (GRPO), Tülu 3 (RLVR), DeepSeek-R1, reward-model overoptimisation and DeepMind's specification gaming.

## Interpretability engine (`src/lib/interpretability/`)

- **Nothing is recorded; Larkspur mini runs live through the KTD5 hooks** (`probe.ts`). Six fuses (`FUSES`): one per head (`h<layer>-<head>`, ablate) and one per MLP (`mlp<layer>`, a model copy with that MLP's `mlp.proj` weight and bias zeroed, cached per set; `src/lib/lm/` is untouched). `run()` is one forward pass with fuses pulled. The model loads through Fine-tuning's loaders (`ModelGate`, `useModel('base')`).
- **Pinned effects** (`data/interpretability/prompts.json`; `tests/unit/interpretability-engine.test.ts` reruns each and fails on drift): chapter 2's held-out counts per head (`headLoss`, over Fine-tuning's held-out copy, `evaluate()`'s windows: of 659 right top-5 guesses, the word-start head L0H0 loses 74, the most) and its one prompt where the word-start fuse bites, labelled one of the few; chapter 3's lens ranks per stage (bar: top 5 after block 1, not after the embedding, for half: 6 of 8); chapter 4's patch flips (`patchGrid`, the swapped word before block 1, the last token after); chapter 5's steering (`steerDirection`, shipping minus billing at the top of layer 2; the target rises from outside the top 5 into it at ×2, a scrap of a word at the strongest); chapter 6's neuron kinds (`kindOf`; bar: half the neurons mix kinds, 199 of 256). A retrain means new prompts, not new copy.
- **Ablation is measured over text, not one prompt** (the user's call when U2's bar failed): one head moves little in a two-layer model; the copy says the work is spread out, and the pinned prompt is labelled as rare.
- **The logit lens** (`lens`) reads three stages: after the embedding, after block 1, the output (`STAGES`); the last equals the model's top 5.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a set of fuses keeps a behaviour when, with every other fuse pulled, its expected token is still top 1. `WINNERS` (every smallest set) is pinned in prompts.json and recomputed by the test; the puzzle accepts any of them.
- **Facts about interpretability** come from `data/interpretability/facts.json` with source and access date; Scaling Monosemanticity is cited through Anthropic's research page (the paper page is too large to fetch).

## Caching engine (`src/lib/caching/`)

- **A discrete-time simulator over a hand-written day, nothing measured** (KTD6, KTD9). `sim.ts` serves `data/caching/trace.json` (Larkspur's shop, 08:00–20:00 in quarter hours: steady rows, flash-sale bursts and the quarter hours at which the depot's copy of each key changes) from one depot and three regional warehouses (north, east, west). Waits come from `data/caching/assumptions.json`, labelled as Larkspur's planning numbers: 12 ms to a warehouse, 60 ms of depot work, 2 ms of round trip per 100 km, and a capacity of 20 trips a quarter hour, past which every trip waits 40 ms more per extra trip. The copy says so wherever a wait shows (`SimNote`).
- **Freshness follows RFC 9111, stale-while-revalidate RFC 5861.** A copy stored in quarter hour s is fresh through s + TTL. TTLs step over `TTLS` (none, 15 min, 1 hour, 4 hours, a day). A trip's reply lands at the end of its quarter hour, so requests in the same quarter hour all see the shelf as it was: without coalescing each miss makes its own trip (the stampede); with it, requests for one key at one warehouse share one trip. Stale-while-revalidate serves an expired copy for `swrTicks` while one trip revalidates.
- **"Stale" means older than the depot's version**, the reader's meaning; "expired" means past the TTL. Purge (prices and photos only; stock counts change with every order and are never purged, an assumption) drops a key at every warehouse when it changes. Versioned names apply to photos only: a change is a new name no shelf holds.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a TTL per kind, purge and versioned names (500 policies), with coalescing on and stale-while-revalidate off. A policy passes when each kind's share of stale reads is within the owner's limit (`data/caching/workbench.json`: prices 0, photos 0, stock 15%, team choices) and scores its hit rate. The puzzle accepts every passing policy tied for the best (`WINNERS`, two today: versioned names add nothing once purge is on).
- **Chapter 6 links `/context/04-order-for-the-cache/` and `/inference/02-kv-cache-at-scale/`**, and those chapters link back to `/caching/`; prompt-cache and KV-cache claims come from the module's own facts.
- **Coverage:** `tests/unit/caching-sim.test.ts` runs every policy and checks the lessons; `caching-workbench.test.ts` checks each chapter's reveal against the runs and that Show me is a winner.
- **Facts about HTTP caching and CDNs** come from `data/caching/facts.json` with source and access date. Fastly's request-collapsing guide is the one CDN document cited, as a source, not a recommendation.

## Databases engine (`src/lib/databases/`)

- **A real B+tree over a generated table, nothing recorded** (KTD7). `table.ts` generates 10,000 orders from a seed (`mulberry32`, `data/databases/assumptions.json`): ids in date order from 1 January to 24 October 2026, customers drawn from 1,000, the last fortnight open and about 1% of older orders returned, 50 to a page (200 pages), beside a 1,000-row customers table (20 pages). `btree.ts` is a B+tree with fan-out 50: bulk-loaded into full pages, insert with page splits, exact lookup, range over key prefixes along the leaf chain, a read counted per page visited and a write per page changed. Keys are number arrays with the order id appended, so every key is unique.
- **Pages are counted, never typed.** A scan reads every table page; an index walks its tree and fetches one table page per row it points to (Larkspur's stated rule, `assumptions.json` `words`). The million rows of chapters 1 and 2 are computed by formula (`scaleTo`: ⌈rows / 50⌉ for a scan, ⌈log₅₀ leaves⌉ + 1 levels plus the row for a lookup), and `databases-btree.test.ts` checks the formula against the measured counts at 10,000 rows. The copy says the million is computed, not stored.
- **The planner is Larkspur's rule, not any database's** (`planner.ts`): from exact per-value statistics it estimates rows (columns assumed independent), estimates an index plan at height + leaves walked + one page per row and a scan at every page, and picks the lower (an index wins a tie). An index is usable only when the request names its leading column; bounds run over leading columns while each is equal to a value, ending at the first range. Chapter 5 cites PostgreSQL's EXPLAIN docs for the real thing.
- **Joins are nested loops** (`join`): the outer request runs through the planner; each outer row scans the customers table, or walks its index and fetches one page.
- **Write cost is measured** (`indexWrites`): a day of new orders (`workload.json` `inserts`) is inserted into a fresh bulk-loaded copy of each index, counting reads and writes (splits included); a write costs one table page plus each index's share.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): any set of the five indexes (32 sets) runs the day's requests (`data/databases/workload.json`, team estimates) through the planner; a set must keep the day's writes within the budget (`budget`, with its reason), and the puzzle accepts every set within it that reads the fewest pages (`WINNERS`, one today).
- **Queries are plain requests**; their SQL lives only in fold-aways (`Sql` in the kit, `requests.ts`).
- **RAG's search index gains no link**: it is a different index.
- **Facts about B-trees, indexes and planners** come from `data/databases/facts.json` with source and access date (Bayer & McCreight; Use The Index, Luke; PostgreSQL's EXPLAIN docs).

## Consistency engine (`src/lib/consistency/`)

- **A replica simulator over a hand-written morning, nothing measured** (KTD10). `sim.ts` runs `data/consistency/day.json` (30 minutes, 19 orders and stock checks, a hinge stock of 7 and one drill press) over three replicas, one per warehouse. A change reaches the others after Larkspur's assumed lag (`assumptions.json` `lagTicks`, slowed so it can be watched; the copy says so). The morning's partition (the line to West down 10:06–10:14) happens only when a chapter cuts the line; messages across it wait and arrive once it is back.
- **Within a minute everything is concurrent**: every read happens before any write, so two orders in the same minute both read the count before either writes. Reads and writes reach R and W replicas, nearest first; a warehouse that can't reach enough refuses. N is 3; R and W step over `QUORUMS`.
- **Two conflict rules** (`RULES`): `lww` (each write stamped by a global counter, every replica keeps the highest: Dynamo's "last write wins") and `primary` (reservations at North: every order decided there one at a time, refused when the ordering warehouse can't reach it; the warehouse learns the new count from the reply). A sale is `lost` when it is off the chain of bases behind the final count.
- **Outputs are counted, never typed**: sold, oversold, lost, refused and sold-out per product, stale reads (a look older than the newest write before that minute) and read-your-writes misses (a customer's look older than their own order). Sticky reads send a customer's looks to the warehouse that took their order. Names in copy (the buyers of the last hinge) are read off `day.json` too.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a setting (R, W, rule) per product, 324 policies, run with the outage on. A policy meets the owner's rule when no product in `workbench.json` `zeroOversell` (the drill press) is oversold; the puzzle accepts every such policy with the fewest refused orders (`WINNERS`, nine today).
- **Coverage:** `tests/unit/consistency-sim.test.ts` pins the plan's scenarios; `consistency-workbench.test.ts` walks every control's domain, checks each reveal against the runs and that Show me lands in `WINNERS`.
- **Facts** (CAP, Dynamo's quorums and last write wins, Jepsen's models, one Raft fact; no consensus internals) come from `data/consistency/facts.json` with source and access date.

## A/B testing engine (`src/lib/ab-testing/`)

- **A seeded visitor simulator, nothing recorded** (KTD11). `sim.ts` sends the same number of shoppers a day to each checkout (an arm); each buys with the arm's true rate and each order comes back with its return rate, drawn as binomials (one uniform each, by inversion) from `mulberry32` with the run's seed. Traffic and rates are Larkspur's labelled assumptions (`data/ab-testing/assumptions.json`); each scenario's true rates and pinned seed are in `data/ab-testing/experiments.json`, written for this site. The copy says simulated shoppers, never a real shop.
- **Rates come from repeated runs, formulas stay folded.** False wins (fixed horizon, daily peeking, twenty colours with and without a correction) and power are counted live over a fixed seed list (`SEEDS`, 1 to `repeats`, 100) and drawn as a tally; the narration shows no formula. `stats.ts` holds the two-proportion z-test (pooled), the interval for B − A (unpooled), Kohavi et al.'s 16σ²/Δ² sample-size rule (fact `sample-size`; the test pins their example) and Bonferroni, shown only in "The maths" fold-aways.
- **Twenty colours share one control**, so the family's false-win rate counted (37 of 100) is below the textbook 1 − 0.95²⁰; the copy only ever shows the counted rate.
- **Pinned runs teach one thing each**: chapter 2's A/A run has a visible gap and no winner, chapter 3's misses at one week and finds the lift at the planned size, chapter 4's peek declares a win the full run does not keep, chapter 5's crowns one colour by luck. `tests/unit/ab-testing-sim.test.ts` and `ab-testing-workbench.test.ts` fail if a seed or rate change breaks one.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): a decision (ship, keep testing, stop) per experiment, 3^experiments plans, each read once at its planned end. The rule: ship when significant, better and the returns guardrail holds; keep testing when not significant and underpowered (fewer shoppers than `NEEDED`); stop otherwise. `WINNERS` is every plan that follows it (one today). The true effects are revealed after the bench, apart from the rule, including the lucky win the rule ships.
- **Facts about controlled experiments** come from `data/ab-testing/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Diffusion engine (`src/lib/diffusion/`)

- **An exact ideal denoiser for pictures, a learned denoiser for points** (KTD8). `drawings.ts` holds twelve exact 32×32 part drawings (hinge, drill, bracket, four each), filled in plain TypeScript so Node and every browser see the same pixels. `schedule.ts` holds the cosine schedule, forward noising (`noised`) and the DDPM step (`stepTo`) both denoisers share; step counts (`STEPS`) and noise levels (`NOISE`) are finite lists.
- **The ideal denoiser runs live** (`ideal.ts`): the posterior mean over the drawings, about 4 ms for 50 steps. One step gives the blurred average; two or more land on a drawing. Chapter 4's version over 24 training points on the hinge outline (`trainingPoints`) can only copy them.
- **The label is soft on purpose** (`LABEL_PRIOR`, 3), departing from KTD8's "restrict its average to one class": the labelled guess weighs the label's drawings 3× the others. A strict label already lands on the right part, which leaves guidance nothing to teach; chapter 5's copy says the soft label is this site's choice. Classifier-free guidance mixes guesses as u + scale·(c − u) (Ho and Salimans' (1 + w)·c − w·u with scale = 1 + w), over `GUIDANCE` (0, 1, 2, 4); a scale other than 0 or 1 costs two predictions a step (`passes`).
- **Chapter 2 runs on the learned 2-D dots**, not on pictures: the ideal denoiser lands on a drawing at two steps, so pictures can't show roughness. Its roughness is `roughness()` in `points.ts`, the dots' distance to the hinge outline.
- **The learned 2-D denoiser runs live in slices** (`points.ts`, the kit's `useLearnedPoints`): each animation frame moves as many dots one step as fit in 8 ms, so there is no long task; with reduced motion only the end shows. Its weights are the language-model kit's 2-D denoiser (below).
- **Pinned outputs** (KTD12, `data/diffusion/pinned.json`): what each pinned seed draws, and chapter 7's measurements per choice. `tests/unit/diffusion-engine.test.ts` reruns them and fails on drift.
- **Chapter 7's workbench** (`workbench.ts`): steps × guidance per use (thumbnail, catalogue image), each use's roughness bar in `data/diffusion/workbench.json` (the team's choices); a use passes when every test picture (3 parts × 8 seeds) is the right part within its bar; cost is predictions per picture. The puzzle accepts every plan tied for the lowest cost (`WINNERS`, two today).
- **Facts about diffusion** come from `data/diffusion/facts.json` with source and access date.

## Language-model kit (`src/lib/lm/`)

Shared by the Tokens, Transformers, Context, Fine-tuning, Structured outputs, Reasoning, Small models, Inference, Multimodal, Voice, Pre-training, Post-training and Interpretability modules. Nothing in it is a live LLM: the browser runs a tokenizer and a tiny model over files built ahead of time.

- **One tokenizer** (`bpe.ts`): a byte-level BPE whose merges (`data/lm/merges.json`, 512: vocab 768) are trained on `data/corpus/` by `pnpm train:bpe`. Training is deterministic (highest count, then lowest pair); the vocab is derived from the merges, never committed. No unknown token. `tests/unit/lm-bpe.test.ts` fails if `merges.json` is stale. 512, not the planned 1,000: of the counts tried (256, 384, 512, 1,000) it reads best at the KTD3 size (real words, the right facts), though 256 scores higher on held-out top-5 because smaller tokens are easier to guess. Changing `N_MERGES` means a full retrain (below).
- **Larkspur mini is trained once in Python; the browser only runs it.** `scripts/train/train.py` (pinned in `requirements.txt`, nanoGPT-shaped, MIT) is run by hand and its outputs are committed. CI never runs Python. The run is seeded, CPU, one thread, deterministic: a retrain gives a byte-identical `model.bin`.
- **Python never tokenizes.** `pnpm encode:lm` (`scripts/encode-for-training.mjs`) writes `data/lm/ids.json`: token ids per document for each split (`train`; `heldout`, every tenth document in sorted order from the sixth, listed again in `meta.json`) and the five golden prompts. The fine-tuning style set sits beside `splits` as `styleSet` (its examples, the sign-off's ids and the chapter prompts the fine-tunes are tried on), not in it, because every split's docs are corpus files.
- **Size ceiling: 2 layers, 2 heads, d_model 32, context 32**, the BPE vocabulary, tied unembedding (about 51k parameters, 100 KB). Weights are Float16 little-endian in `public/data/lm/model.bin` (≤ 150 KB), laid out in `public/data/lm/meta.json` with the config, training settings, loss curve and held-out scores. The model is tiny and overfit (held-out top-5 about 44%): held-out scores are reported, not gated, and the copy says so. The gate is per chapter: every prompt a chapter shows must have its expected next token in the model's top 5. d_model 64 breaks the payload budget: stop and ask, never a routine fallback.
- **The forward pass** (`model.ts`) is plain TypeScript: LayerNorm, causal softmax attention (max subtracted before `exp`), tanh-GELU MLP (no `erf` in JS). It returns logits at every position and every attention matrix. A model may carry an `adapter` (LoRA: per adapted matrix, A and B scaled alpha / rank, added to the frozen weight's output); without one the pass is unchanged. A prompt longer than the context reads its last 32 tokens and returns `truncated: true`; copy that shows a long prompt says so. `evaluate()` gives held-out loss and top-1 / top-5 the way `train.py` does. `load.ts` fetches and decodes the weights the way RAG's index loads (loading state, a failed load retries), for any of three choices (`Weights`): `base` (`model.bin`), `finetuned` (`finetuned.bin`, the same layout) and `lora` (`model.bin` plus `lora.bin`, laid out by `meta.json` `lora.tensors`).
- **Goldens come from the shipped weights.** `train.py` rounds the weights to Float16, casts them back, and computes every golden (`tests/fixtures/lm/goldens.json`: last-position logits and attention for five prompts, held-out scores) in float64 from them. `tests/unit/lm-model.test.ts` checks the TS pass against them with a tolerance (max |Δlogit| < 1e-4, same top-5, attention rows sum to 1), never bit-exact.
- **The student** (KTD1 of the eight-modules plan, for Small models and Inference): `train.py --distill` never retrains the base. It distils `model.bin` into a smaller model (1 layer, 2 heads, d_model 16, d_mlp 64, context 32, the same vocabulary, tied unembedding: 16k parameters) on the base's next-token distribution softened at temperature 2, plus a 0.1 weight on the true token, over the training split. It ships as `public/data/lm/student.bin` (Float16, ≤ 40 KB), with its own config, layout, loss curve, held-out scores and `agreement` (held-out positions where its greedy next token is the base's) in `meta.json` `student`, and its goldens in `goldens.json` `student`. `load.ts` loads it as `Weights` `'student'`; `agreement()` in `model.ts` recomputes the share live. Agreement under 0.40 is a stop: the fallback is 2 layers at d_model 16, never a wider student. `tests/unit/lm-student.test.ts` checks the goldens, the size, the agreement, the loader, and that the base, the fine-tunes and their meta and goldens are unchanged.
- **Hooks** (KTD5 of the eight-new-modules plan, for Interpretability): `forward(model, prompt, hooks?)` takes optional hooks, looked up once per layer or head, never in the inner loops. `ablate` zeroes a head's output before the projection (the bias still applies, and `attention` still reports the head's pattern); `residuals` returns the stream after the embedding and after each block; `mlp` returns each MLP neuron's activation after GELU; `patch` replaces one position's residual and `steer` adds a vector to every position's, both at the top of a layer (`n_layer` means before `lnf`; positions count after the 32-token truncation). `unembed()` is the logit lens: `lnf`, then the tied unembedding. With no hooks the pass is unchanged (`tests/unit/lm-hooks.test.ts`; the goldens hold). `data/interpretability/neurons.json` holds each neuron's five highest-activating tokens over the training split; `tests/unit/interpretability-neurons.test.ts` recomputes it every run and `WRITE_NEURONS=1` rewrites it. Silencing one head moves little in a two-layer model (the word-start head, the strongest, drops about 11% of right top-5 guesses over held-out text and breaks none of Transformers' prompts), so Interpretability measures ablation over text, not on a pinned prompt.
- **Checkpoints and variant runs** (KTD3, for Pre-training): `train.py --checkpoints` reruns the base run (one shared loop, `pretrain()`, whose hook copies Float16 weights and draws no randomness) and writes steps 0, 100, 250, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 3000 to `public/data/pre-training/ckpt-<step>.bin` (model.bin's layout, 817 KB in all, capped at 1 MB), with their layout, settings and float64 scores in `public/data/pre-training/meta.json`, never the base `meta.json`. The last checkpoint is `model.bin` byte for byte; a drifted rerun stops and writes nothing. `train.py --variants` reruns the base on four data mixes, `clean` (must reproduce `model.bin`), `duplicated` (one policy document ten times), `leaked` (Evals' twelve questions with their reference answers) and `half` (every other document), into `data/pre-training/runs.json` (curves, scores, each run's lowest held-out step, greedy samples as ids, `earlyMinimum`; only `half` has a real early minimum, at step 600). No variant weights ship. The duplicated document, leaked questions and pinned prompts are `ids.json` `preTraining` (`pnpm encode:lm`). `load.ts` loads a checkpoint on demand as `Weights` `'ckpt-<step>'`; a missing step rejects into the retry path. `tests/unit/pre-training-runs.test.ts` checks bytes, sizes, losses, runs and the loader, and pins every other weight file.
- **The 2-D denoiser** (KTD8, for Diffusion models) is trained once in Python like Larkspur mini: `scripts/train/train_diffusion.py` (seeded, CPU, one thread, byte-identical on rerun) trains a small noise-predicting MLP on points from a hinge outline into `public/data/diffusion/points.bin` (Float16, ≤ 60,000 bytes) and `meta.json` (layout, schedule, loss curve, outline, goldens). Rerun with `uv run --no-project --python 3.12 --with-requirements scripts/train/requirements.txt scripts/train/train_diffusion.py`, then `pnpm test`.
- **Retrain** (only after editing `data/corpus/`, the merges or `train.py`): `pnpm train:bpe` if the merges changed, `pnpm encode:lm`, then `uv run --no-project --python 3.12 --with-requirements scripts/train/requirements.txt scripts/train/train.py` (details in `scripts/train/README.md`), then `pnpm test`, and commit `merges.json`, `ids.json`, `model.bin`, `meta.json` and `goldens.json` together. Any chapter text quoting the model's predictions changes with it. A base retrain means rerunning `train.py --finetune`, `--distill`, `--checkpoints` and `--variants` too (all start from or reproduce `model.bin`), committing `finetuned.bin`, `lora.bin`, `student.bin`, the checkpoints and `runs.json` with the rest, and updating the sha256 pins in `tests/unit/pre-training-runs.test.ts` and `data/interpretability/neurons.json` (`WRITE_NEURONS=1`).

## Tooling

- **pnpm 11** (`packageManager`), **Node 22.23.1** (`.node-version`; Astro 7 needs ≥ 22.22). Install scripts of `onnxruntime-node` and `protobufjs` are off in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` (not needed).
- **The dev server has its own Vite cache (`node_modules/.vite-dev`).** `astro build` and `astro check` pre-bundle production React into the shared cache, which made a running dev server crash with `_jsxDEV is not a function` (figures flashed and vanished). Keep the `dev-cache` integration in `astro.config.mjs`.
- **Imports from `public/` (Larkspur mini's `meta.json`) go through the `public-json` plugin in dev.** Vite would serve them as `/public/...` URLs and warn "Assets in public directory cannot be imported from JavaScript"; the plugin loads them as modules. Builds bundle them as before.
- Dev-only pages (`/dev/blocks`, `/dev/canvas`, `/dev/runner`, `/dev/flat`, `/dev/quiz`, `/dev/recap`, `/dev/warmup`, `/dev/agents`, `/dev/evals`, `/dev/guardrails`, `/dev/tokens`, `/dev/transformers`, `/dev/context`, `/dev/fine-tuning`, `/dev/structured-outputs`, `/dev/reasoning`, `/dev/small-models`, `/dev/inference`, `/dev/observability`, `/dev/sandboxing`, `/dev/multimodal`, `/dev/voice`, `/dev/pre-training`, `/dev/post-training`, `/dev/interpretability`, `/dev/caching`, `/dev/databases`, `/dev/consistency`, `/dev/ab-testing`, `/dev/diffusion`, `/dev/og/*`) exist in `astro dev` or with `DEV_PAGES=1`; production builds leave them out.
- **Biome lints and formats** (`biome.json`): 2 spaces, single quotes, 120 columns. `pnpm format` rewrites; `pnpm lint` (`biome ci`) fails on any lint or format diagnostic. Rules that fight the house style or misfire here (non-null `!`, SVG roles, Astro template imports, `fit`/`after` read as test helpers) are off; a11y is covered by axe in e2e. A real exception gets a `biome-ignore` with its reason, never a rule switched off for one site. Dependabot opens one grouped PR a month for npm and one for Actions.
- The RAG index in `public/data/index/` is committed. Only rebuild it (`pnpm build:index`) after editing `data/corpus/` or `data/questions/`, never on a host.

## Checks before you call it done

```bash
pnpm check       # types: 0 errors, 0 warnings
pnpm lint        # Biome lint + format: 0 diagnostics (pnpm format fixes)
pnpm test        # unit (Vitest)
pnpm test:e2e    # every chapter played through, axe, keyboard only, reduced motion, phone, offline, share images
```

Look at the pages you changed (desktop and 390px wide), not just the tests. New behaviour gets a test.

`tests/unit/registry.test.ts` walks `MODULES` and the files: it fails, naming what is missing, when a live module lacks a level, an author, a page, the Colorfigures credit on its intro, a share image, recall items for every chapter (exactly one pinned) or an e2e spec, or when module ids, paths or share images collide. CI (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`) runs all four commands on every PR, e2e in six shards. `E2E_PORT` moves the e2e preview server off 4399, so worktrees can run e2e side by side.

## Adding a module

1. Plan it first (concept, level, story, chapter list, sources; see `CONTRIBUTING.md`).
2. `pnpm new-module <id> "<Title>"` copies `templates/module/` (registry entry, intro, chapter route, recap, one chapter, e2e spec), appends the module to the end of `MODULES` (removing its planned line, if it has one) and refuses an id that exists. `templates/` sits outside `src/`, so Astro never builds it.
3. Write it by `docs/writing-a-module.md`; `pnpm test` (the registry test) lists what is still missing. A new module is picture-first from its first commit: the copy budgets, `act` for every instruction, `say` callouts on its scene's exported parts, `Cite` for sources, and the fact rules (framing that names whose figure and when; dated, re-checked sources) all apply. The copy-budget, callout-target and opening checks read the registry, so they hold a new module to these without any list to edit.
4. Reuse `src/lib/iso/` for pictures and label pills; keep the Colorfigures credit. Then `pnpm og`.
